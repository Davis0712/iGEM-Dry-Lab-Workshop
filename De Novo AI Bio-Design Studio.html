HKU iGEM · De novo design studio

Design a better protein to switch off inflammation.

A chronic wound keeps triggering an immune alarm. The alarm travels through a receptor called MD2.

A peptide called TAP2 already blocks MD2 and helps wounds heal — but it is short-lived, moderately strong, and can provoke an immune response. Our job is not to invent from nothing: it is to design a better TAP2 using generative AI, then run it through the same filters a real drug candidate faces.

ScThe patch of surface a binder touches is the epitope

A binding surface is a question about geometry. Every residue on MD2 has a position in 3D space; whether a designed residue can reach it is a distance calculation. When we say 'A82 and A85 are the hotspot', we mean: any binder that covers this pair will block the pocket.

contact(i, j) ⟺ ‖ xi − yj ‖ < rc
xi, yj  atom positions of target and binder
rc  contact distance, about 5 Å
in practice  a whole matrix of pairwise distances decides the interface

What the models are actually doing

RFdiffusion starts from random noise shaped like a protein and removes the noise step by step until a plausible backbone appears — the same idea as an image generator, in 3D.

ProteinMPNN keeps that backbone fixed and solves a puzzle: which amino acid sequence would most likely fold into it?

10 candidate designs

They all fit the hotspot. They do not all fold, grip, or stay quiet inside a human body. That is what the funnel is for.

ScA short chain like this is a peptide; AI-designed binders are often called de novo binders

RFdiffusion is trained to reverse a noising process. If you add noise to a real protein structure over many steps, you get pure static; the model learns to run that backwards. Generation starts from static and ends at a protein.

εθ  the network's guess at the noise

the motif  positions A82 / A85 are held fixed at every step

Stage 2.1 · fold quality (pLDDT) · 5 min Done

Throw away anything that won't fold

A designed sequence only works if it actually curls into a stable shape. AlphaFold gives every design a per-residue confidence called pLDDT, scored 0–100. Keep anything at 70 or above. Discard the rest.

iClick a row to mark it for discarding. Green rows are the ones you keep.
Click a row to mark it for discarding.
8 of 10 survived
pLDDT < 70=the model isn't sure the shape is real

Confidence is not the same as quality — but a design nobody can fold has no chance at all, and it is the cheapest thing to check first.

ScFiltering cheapest-first is a funnel; each stage is a selection criterion

pLDDT is a per-residue confidence. AlphaFold was trained to predict how close its own prediction would land, so the number is not a guess about the protein — it is a calibrated statement about the model.

pLDDTi ≈ 100 × P( errori < 2 Å )
i  one residue
> 90  confident enough for most work
< 70  the backbone is probably not real

Stage 2.2 · docking confidence (ipTM) · 5 min Done

Does it actually grip MD2?

Boltz-1 folds the binder together with MD2 and reports ipTM, a 0–1 score for how confident it is about the interface. Keep anything at 0.60 or above.

iThese 8 all fold. Now we ask whether they hold on.
7 survived
ipTM < 0.60=the model can't commit to the interface

A binder that folds but doesn't hold on is just a floating peptide. It would drift away before it could block anything.

ScWhen two molecules stick together they co-fold; the shape they make is a complex

ipTM measures whether the model is confident about the interface between two chains, separately from whether each chain folds correctly. It is a normalised score, so it can be compared across different complexes.

ipTM = 11 + d̄interface / d0
d̄  average distance error at the interface
d0  a normalising length that grows with complex size
0.60  the usual bar for 'worth testing in the lab'

Stage 2.3 · human-proteome similarity screen (MMseqs2) · 5 min Done

Does it resemble a human sequence?

MMseqs2 compares every candidate against the human proteome. A significant match does not prove off-target binding, but it is a useful flag for closer review. For this simplified bootcamp funnel, hold back any candidate with one or more flagged matches.

iMMseqs2 detects sequence similarity, not binding. A hit is a review flag — not proof that the peptide is dangerous.
5 survived
significant match=review before advancing

This inexpensive screen helps prioritise candidates for closer analysis before experimental work. A match is a caution flag; zero matches is not a guarantee of specificity.

ScSequence similarity is a useful screen, but functional off-target binding needs additional modelling or experiments

MMseqs2 looks for local alignments between your peptide and every human protein, then reports how surprising each match is. The E-value is the number of matches this good you would expect to find by chance in a database this size.

E = K · m · n · e−λS
S  the alignment score
K, λ  constants fitted to the scoring matrix
m, n  lengths of the two sequences being compared

ScShaking a molecule with physics for a while is molecular dynamics

Molecular dynamics integrates equations of motion for the atoms under a chosen force field. RMSD summarises average structural displacement after each frame is aligned to the reference structure.

RMSD(t) = √1N Σi=1N ‖ rialigned(t) − ri(0) ‖²
N  number of atoms compared
low plateau  limited drift from the reference
persistent rise  a warning sign that merits closer structural analysis

−6.0 weak−8.0 target−10.0 very strong
How UCB chose that

Upper Confidence Bound scores every position as μ + c √(ln N / n) — how good it looks so far (μ), plus a bonus for being under-explored (the square-root term). Then it picks the highest score. That is how you balance trying new things against using what already works.

Strong enough to build
ΔG ≤ −8.0=passes this activity's affinity target

Every mutation we tried was tested by a model, not a pipette. A wet lab would take weeks to test this many variants — the model narrows thousands of options down to a handful worth testing for real.

ScSearching a space by balancing exploration and exploitation is Bayesian optimisation

Every mutation is a gamble: it might improve binding, or it might not. Upper Confidence Bound is the classic way to gamble well — try the options that look good, but also try the ones you know least about, because your estimate of them is uncertain.

ai = μi − c √ln Nni
μi  best result seen at position i so far
ni  how many times i has been tried
N  total tries across all positions
c  exploration weight — bigger c means more curiosity

Stage 4.2 · lab compliance · 5 min Done

Clear the safety check

Before any DNA is ordered, three questions. Read the explanation under each option before you choose — some of them sound reasonable but protect the wrong thing.

1. Assume a non-pathogenic Risk Group 1 E. coli strain, a sequence with no identified hazardous function, routine small-scale work, and local approval. Which containment level is normally appropriate?

2. Before ordering the DNA, what should you screen the sequence against?

3. Your peptide is meant for a wound dressing. Which result would stop you from proceeding?

Risk is not a yes/no property. Each computational check answers a different question, and passing them only supports advancement to further testing — it does not certify safety or clinical efficacy.

advance ⟺ pLDDT ≥ 70 ∧ ipTM ≥ 0.60 ∧ similarity flags = 0 ∧ strong MHC-I signals = 0
∧  all bootcamp gates must hold
advance  prioritise for further simulation, risk assessment and experiments — not approve for clinical use
